# Ex.No.-1.1 – Design and Draft the Given 2D Sketches in Modelling Software

## AIM

To design and draft the given two-dimensional engineering sketch with accurate dimensions using Autodesk Fusion 360 and convert the completed sketch into a three-dimensional model.

## REQUIREMENTS

1. Autodesk Fusion 360 (Education License).
2. Windows 10 or a compatible Windows operating system.
3. 16 GB RAM (recommended system configuration).
4. Stable internet connection for accessing Autodesk Fusion services.
5. A computer system capable of running 3D CAD modelling software.

## PROCEDURE

### 1. Creating a New Design

Launch Autodesk Fusion 360 and create a new design using the File menu. Ensure that the Design workspace is active before starting the modelling process. Save the document with an appropriate experiment name for easy identification.

<img width="1056" height="682" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2c4de79e-d337-43e7-8050-b8057d86856b" />

### 2. Selecting the Sketch Plane

Select the Create Sketch command from the toolbar. Choose a suitable reference plane (XY, XZ or YZ) according to the orientation of the given engineering drawing. The selected plane acts as the base for constructing the two-dimensional geometry.

<img width="540" height="691" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/dfba48b5-bbd0-404b-a184-7a20478a1d43" />

### 3. Constructing the Main Sketch Geometry

Use the available sketching tools, such as Line, Circle, Arc and other geometric commands, to construct the required shape. Begin by creating the main outline and then develop the internal features according to the reference drawing.

<img width="345" height="533" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a10d2017-a246-427b-80ab-2e00fae2e51a" />

### 4. Creating the Outer Circular Features

Four circular features were constructed at the upper, lower, left and right portions of the design. Smaller concentric circles were added within the outer circular sections to represent the required holes.

The Circle command was used to create the circular geometry, ensuring that the features were positioned according to the reference drawing.

<img width="696" height="628" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/22f3a4b8-a207-4468-aaaf-ce69a3fced80" />

### 5. Developing the Connecting Curves and Inner Profile

The outer circular sections were connected using suitable curved sketch geometries. Additional circles, arcs and connecting lines were used to develop the central pattern.

The curved boundaries were adjusted to maintain smooth transitions between the different sections of the design.

### 6. Applying Geometric Constraints and Modifying Features

Geometric constraints were applied wherever required to maintain the alignment and relationships between sketch elements. Construction lines and reference points were used to maintain the intended symmetry.

Sketch modification commands such as Trim and other appropriate editing tools were used to remove unwanted portions and refine the overall profile.

<img width="338" height="482" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5297c124-d613-43ac-8160-476a9f7013a3" />

### 7. Applying Dimensions to the Sketch

The Sketch Dimension command was used to specify the required measurements. Diameters, radii and linear distances were assigned according to the given engineering drawing.

The following dimensions were incorporated into the sketch:

**Circular Dimensions:**
- Upper outer circle: Ø32 mm
- Upper inner circle: Ø20 mm
- Central circular feature: Ø40 mm
- Inner circular feature: Ø12 mm
- Large reference arc: Ø128 mm
- Right-side circular feature: Ø20 mm
- Inner right circular feature: Ø14 mm
- Bottom outer circular feature: Ø20 mm
- Bottom inner circular feature: Ø10 mm

**Linear Dimensions:**
- Upper vertical distance: 65 mm
- Lower vertical distance: 50 mm
- Right-side vertical distance: 24 mm
- Horizontal distance: 47 mm

The dimensions were checked to ensure that the sketch maintained the required proportions and geometric arrangement.

### 8. Completing and Verifying the 2D Sketch

After completing the geometry and dimensioning process, the sketch was inspected in the Front view. The outer boundary, internal circular features, connecting curves and central pattern were checked.

The Finish Sketch command was selected to finalise the two-dimensional sketch.

<img width="186" height="218" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/65ebfa87-a068-4357-a485-1e4d98ce4cfa" />

### 9. Converting the 2D Sketch into a 3D Model

After completing the two-dimensional sketch, the design was prepared for three-dimensional modelling. The appropriate closed sketch profile was used to generate the solid component by applying the required 3D modelling operation.

The internal openings and outer profile were preserved to maintain the intended geometry of the component.

### 10. Inspecting the Completed 3D Model

The completed component was viewed in an isometric orientation to examine its overall shape and thickness.

The four outer circular openings, central shaped opening and curved outer boundaries were inspected to verify the appearance of the three-dimensional model.

The completed design was saved in Autodesk Fusion 360 for further drawing preparation.

### 11. Preparing the Engineering Drawing

After completing the 3D model, the Drawing workspace was used to prepare the engineering drawing. The drawing was generated from the completed design using the appropriate drawing creation option.

### 12. Applying Dimensions and Finalising the Drawing

The Dimension tool was used to display the necessary measurements in the engineering drawing. The placement and readability of the dimensions were checked to ensure that the drawing represented the designed component accurately.
